Social Value in UK Tenders: How to Score Full Marks on PPN 06/20

A comprehensive guide to understanding the UK Social Value Model, meeting mandatory PPN 06/20 requirements, avoiding the generic CSR trap, and crafting high-scoring commitments backed by verifiable metrics.

By Alioth Digital Studios Published: 25 Nov 2026 Last updated: 25 Nov 2026 11-13 min read

In UK public procurement, winning a contract no longer depends solely on technical capability and competitive pricing. Since the implementation of Procurement Policy Note 06/20 (PPN 06/20), social value is an explicit, mandatory evaluation requirement across central government, the NHS, defence, education, and local authorities.

With social value carrying a minimum 10% weighting—and frequently reaching 15% to 20% of the total award score—it regularly determines the difference between winning a multi-million-pound contract and finishing in second place. Yet, it remains the single section where bidders lose the most marks.

The Core Rule: Public sector evaluators will award zero or minimal marks to generic company-wide Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) policies. To score top marks, your social value commitments must be contract-specific, delivered in the buyer’s local geographic area, and offer genuine additionality with measurable numerical targets.

1. What is Social Value in UK Procurement?

Social value refers to the wider positive impact an organisation creates for society, the local economy, and the environment through the delivery of a public contract. While the Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012 initially required commissioners to consider economic, social, and environmental wellbeing, PPN 06/20 made explicit evaluation and quantitative scoring compulsory for central government procurement.

Rather than treating community benefits as an optional goodwill gesture, public buyers use their procurement spend as an economic lever to address systemic challenges, such as local unemployment, the digital skills divide, supplier diversity, and the transition to Net Zero.

2. Understanding PPN 06/20 and the Social Value Model

Procurement Policy Note 06/20 (PPN 06/20) introduced the standardized Social Value Model. This framework establishes consistent criteria, reporting metrics, and evaluation scoring across all central government departments, executive agencies, and non-departmental public bodies (NDPBs).

Key requirements of the policy include:

  • Mandatory Minimum Weighting: Social value must carry at least 10% of the overall evaluation weighting. It cannot be combined with broader quality criteria or diluted into standard service-delivery answers.
  • No Pass/Fail Evaluation: Social value cannot be assessed on a simple compliance tick-box basis; it must be scored qualitatively from zero to maximum points against published criteria.
  • Contractual Inclusion: All commitments made in the winning tender response are written directly into the final contract schedules as legally binding Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).

3. The 5 Themes of the Social Value Model

The UK Social Value Model is structured around five national priority themes. In any given tender pack, the contracting authority will select one or two themes that align directly with the subject matter of the contract.

Theme 1: Covid-19 Recovery

Focuses on helping local communities and businesses recover from the economic, health, and social impacts of the pandemic. Objectives include supporting individuals to return to sustainable employment, workplace health and safety adaptations, and community cohesion initiatives.

Theme 2: Tackling Economic Inequality

One of the most frequently selected themes across IT, construction, and professional services tenders. It centers on two Model Award Criteria (MAC):

  • MAC 2.1 (Entrepreneurship & Economic Growth): Creating employment and training opportunities, particularly for disadvantaged groups, young people, and individuals in areas of high deprivation. Creating opportunities for small-and-medium enterprises (SMEs), Voluntary, Community and Social Enterprises (VCSEs), and mutuals in the supply chain.
  • MAC 2.2 (Employment & Skills): Developing skills, offering accredited apprenticeships (Levels 2 to 7), addressing technical skills shortages, and supporting educational outreach.

Theme 3: Fighting Climate Change

Focuses on environmental protection and supporting the UK's legal commitment to achieve Net Zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. Evaluated under MAC 3.1 (often aligned with PPN 06/21 Carbon Reduction Plans on major contracts), requirements include carbon reduction plans, energy efficiency, waste minimization, circular economy practices, and sustainable supply chain logistics.

Theme 4: Equal Opportunity

Aims to eliminate employment barriers for underrepresented groups through two core criteria:

  • MAC 4.1 (Reducing the Disability Employment Gap): Increasing the representation of disabled people in the contract workforce, offering accessible recruitment pathways, and providing specialist workplace adjustments.
  • MAC 4.2 (Tackling Workforce Inequality): Demonstrating transparent, fair recruitment, closing gender and ethnic pay disparities, and supporting upward social mobility.

Theme 5: Wellbeing

Focuses on improving the physical and mental health of the contract workforce and local communities (MAC 5.1). Commitments include mental health first aid training, inclusive working conditions, and partnerships with local community health initiatives.

4. Why Bidders Fail: The Corporate CSR Trap

When reviewing unsuccessful bids during our compliance and quality assurance reviews, the most common reason for low social value scores is the "Corporate CSR Trap."

Bidders frequently copy and paste excerpts from their corporate website, reciting general environmental policies, company charity days, or national carbon offset certificates. Evaluators grade these answers poorly because they violate the foundational principles of public procurement:

  • Lack of Contract Relevance: The commitments are company-wide and would happen regardless of whether the buyer awards this specific contract.
  • No Additionality: Social value must represent additional benefit generated as a direct consequence of the public funding allocated to the contract.
  • Geographic Disconnect: A company based in London bidding on a healthcare framework in Manchester cannot offer volunteering hours in London; the benefit must impact the contracting authority's local community.
  • Absence of Verifiable Metrics: Phrases such as "we will strive to support local charities" or "we encourage staff diversity" offer no tangible commitment that an evaluator can score or a contract manager can enforce.

5. The "MAC" Winning Structure: How to Write Responses that Score 5/5

To score top marks against Crown Commercial Service (CCS) frameworks and local authority evaluation matrices, structure your answer using the proven MAC Formula (Method, Additionality, Commitment):

The 4-Part Winning Social Value Framework

  1. Context & Local Alignment: Demonstrate clear understanding of the buyer’s published social priorities and the specific demographic or economic challenges of the contract area.
  2. The Method (What & How): Detail exactly what program or initiative you will implement, who will deliver it, and which local third-party partners (e.g. local colleges, Jobcentre Plus, local charities) you have engaged.
  3. Additionality (Why it Matters): Explicitly explain why this benefit is directly created by this contract and exceeds business-as-usual operations.
  4. Metrics & Milestones (The Quantitative Commitment): Provide concrete, unambiguous numbers, delivery timetables, and monitoring mechanisms.

6. Worked Tender Example: Tackling Economic Inequality

Below is a practical comparison showing how to transform a vague answer into a high-scoring response under Theme 2 (MAC 2.1). This example is illustrative for a UK software and IT services tender.

Tender Question: "Please describe the commitment your organisation will make to tackle economic inequality, support local employment, and upskill individuals in the contract delivery region over the 3-year contract term. (Maximum 500 words · 10% weighting)"

The Weak Answer (Score: 1/5 · Unacceptable / Limited Evidence)

"Alioth Tech is committed to corporate responsibility. We promote equal opportunity and support local communities. Throughout the contract, we will endeavor to hire locally wherever possible and encourage our staff to mentor young people. We work with local suppliers and have a strong diversity policy. We also provide internal training courses to help staff improve their skills."

Evaluator critique: Zero additionality. No named partners, no timescales, no metrics. Words like "endeavor" and "wherever possible" carry zero contractual commitment.

The Winning Answer (Score: 5/5 · Outstanding / Comprehensive Commitment)

1. Alignment & Partner Engagement: To support economic regeneration within the Authority’s target wards, we have partnered with Skills Forward Birmingham and the local Jobcentre Plus to establish a structured 3-year digital skills and employment pipeline directly funded through this contract.

2. Core Commitments & Quantitative Metrics:

  • 2 New Level-4 Apprenticeships: We commit to recruiting two full-time Junior Cloud Engineers from candidates residing in postcode areas B1–B12 who are currently not in employment, education, or training (NEET). Recruitment commences within 60 days of contract award.
  • SME Supply Chain Ringfencing: A minimum of 22% of secondary contract expenditure (estimated at £140,000 across Year 1–3) will be subcontracted directly to local SMEs and VCSE suppliers within the region for quality assurance and field testing services.
  • 30 Hours of Digital Mentorship: Our Lead Software Architect will deliver six half-day workshops at South & City College Birmingham, providing 45 students with industry-standard Python and cloud deployment training.

3. Governance, Measurement & Reporting: Delivery will be overseen by our dedicated Social Value Manager, Sarah Jenkins. Progress against our promised metrics will be tracked monthly via the Social Value Portal and formally submitted as a standing agenda item at each quarterly contract review meeting. If an apprentice departs, a replacement will be recruited within 45 days.

Why this scores full marks: The evaluator has clear numbers to input into their scoring matrix, verifiable third-party delivery partners, defined geographic focus, and zero ambiguity regarding enforceability.

7. How Social Value is Monitored and Contracted

Winning bidders must be prepared to deliver what they write. Public authorities enforce commitments through contract clauses backed by reporting platforms and frameworks such as the National TOMs Framework (Themes, Outcomes and Measures).

Your contract manager will require evidence of delivery, including:

  • Signed apprenticeship contracts and college enrollment verification.
  • Invoices demonstrating tier-2 spend with registered SMEs or VCSEs.
  • Attendance registers, agendas, and feedback forms from community workshops.
  • Audited carbon emissions data and waste transfer notes.

Failure to meet commitments can trigger financial remedies, service credits, or low performance marks that directly damage your capability to win future public sector bids.

8. Social Value under the UK Procurement Act 2023

As the UK transitions under the Procurement Act 2023, public procurement rules are shifting from the legacy EU concept of "Most Economically Advantageous Tender" (MEAT) to "Most Advantageous Tender" (MAT), detailed in the official Cabinet Office guidance on assessing competitive tenders.

This statutory change gives contracting authorities explicit legal backing to look beyond pure direct cost and place greater evaluation weight on wider public benefit, social value, and local economic resilience. For suppliers, mastering social value is no longer a niche bid writing skill—it is central to UK commercial competitiveness.

9. 7-Point Social Value Pre-Submission Checklist

Before submitting your response on Find a Tender, Contracts Finder, or a local procurement portal, check your social value commitments against this 7-point quality checklist:

  • Criteria Match: Does the response directly address the specific Model Award Criteria (e.g. MAC 2.1 or MAC 3.1) named in the tender pack?
  • Local Geography: Are the proposed benefits delivered in the buyer’s operational area, not just your head office location?
  • Additionality: Are the activities uniquely generated by this contract, rather than existing company-wide policies?
  • Hard Numbers: Have you replaced subjective promises ("we will try to") with precise numbers (hours, headcount, spend, percentages)?
  • Delivery Partner: Have you identified credible local organizations, colleges, or charities who will assist in delivering the initiative?
  • Governance & Accountability: Have you named the responsible lead and detailed how progress will be measured and reported?
  • Cross-Check with Pricing: Are any costs associated with delivering your social value commitments accurately accounted for in your commercial schedule?

Conclusion & Professional Support

Social value should never be an afterthought tacked onto the end of a proposal. Approached strategically, it is the most reliable section to outscore larger competitors who rely on generic corporate boilerplate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PPN 06/20 in UK government tenders?

PPN 06/20 (Procurement Policy Note 06/20) is UK Cabinet Office policy mandating that central government departments, executive agencies, and non-departmental public bodies explicitly evaluate social value in all major procurements using a standardized Social Value Model.

How much is social value weighted in public sector bids?

Under PPN 06/20, social value must carry a minimum evaluation weighting of 10% of the total score. Many contracting authorities, including NHS trusts and local councils, weight social value between 15% and 20%.

What are the five themes of the UK Social Value Model?

The five themes are: Covid-19 recovery, Tackling economic inequality, Fighting climate change, Equal opportunity, and Wellbeing. Buyers select relevant Model Award Criteria (MAC) from these themes based on contract scope.

What is the difference between CSR and Social Value in tenders?

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) covers general company-wide charitable giving or policies. Social value in tenders must be contract-specific, delivered locally in the contract area, measurable, and additional to statutory minimums.

How are social value responses evaluated and scored?

Evaluators score responses against published evaluation grids (typically 0 to 5 or 0 to 10). Full marks require a detailed delivery method, clear milestones, named owners, identifiable partner organizations, and verifiable quantitative metrics.

Are social value commitments legally binding in the contract?

Yes. Winning tender commitments are incorporated into the contract terms as formal Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). Suppliers must report on delivery progress, and failure to fulfill commitments can result in financial penalties or breach of contract.
