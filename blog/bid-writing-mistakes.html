Top 5 Bid Writing Mistakes

Avoid common bid writing mistakes with practical steps to follow tender instructions, tailor your responses, provide evidence, address scoring criteria and allow time for review.

By Alioth Digital Studios Published: 15 Oct 2026 Last updated: 24 Oct 2026 5 min read

Winning contracts requires more than offering competitive prices and quality services. Understanding the most common bid writing mistakes can help businesses prepare stronger tender submissions, demonstrate their value, and improve their chances of success. Whether you are responding to a public sector procurement opportunity or a private sector contract, a well-structured bid can make a significant difference.

Many businesses lose valuable opportunities because their submissions fail to address evaluation criteria, provide sufficient evidence, or communicate their strengths effectively. By identifying these common errors and following proven bid writing practices, you can produce clearer, more persuasive tender responses.

1. Failing to Follow the Tender Requirements

One of the most common bid writing mistakes is overlooking the buyer's instructions. Tender documents often contain specific requirements covering submission deadlines, word counts, mandatory documents, formatting, and evaluation criteria.

Missing even one essential requirement could affect your score or result in your submission being rejected.

How to avoid this mistake:

  • Read the complete tender documentation before starting.
  • Create a compliance checklist covering every requirement.
  • Highlight mandatory questions, supporting documents, and deadlines.
  • Assign responsibilities to team members and track progress.
  • Review the final submission against the original instructions.

A systematic approach helps ensure your bid is complete, compliant, and submitted on time. If you need help planning your response, our bid planning and submission service can guide you through every stage.

2. Using Generic and Unpersuasive Responses

Another frequent problem is submitting generic content that could apply to almost any organisation. Buyers want to understand how your business will meet their specific needs, manage risks, and deliver measurable results.

A generic response may describe your experience but fail to explain why it matters to the buyer.

How to Make Your Bid More Persuasive

  • Tailor every response to the contract requirements.
  • Demonstrate your understanding of the buyer's challenges and explain how your proposed solution addresses them.
  • Where appropriate, include relevant case studies, measurable outcomes, customer feedback, and examples of previous successful projects.
  • For additional guidance, consult the UK government's official guidance on public procurement.

3. Failing to Provide Evidence and Examples

Making claims without supporting evidence can weaken your credibility. Statements such as "we provide excellent customer service" or "we consistently deliver high-quality projects" are less convincing without proof.

Buyers need confidence that your organisation can deliver the promised results.

How to avoid this mistake (support your claims with relevant evidence, including):

  • Case studies demonstrating successful project delivery.
  • Key performance indicators and measurable outcomes.
  • Customer testimonials and relevant references.
  • Industry qualifications, accreditations, and certifications.
  • Examples of effective risk management and problem-solving.

Use specific, relevant evidence to demonstrate your capabilities. Make sure every example directly supports the question being evaluated.

4. Overlooking the Evaluation Criteria

A well-written submission can still underperform if it does not address the buyer's scoring criteria. Tender questions are designed to assess particular capabilities, and failing to answer them directly can cost valuable marks.

Some businesses focus too heavily on describing their company instead of explaining how they will meet the contract requirements.

How to Align Your Bid With Scoring Criteria

  • Study the evaluation methodology before drafting your answers.
  • Identify the key requirements in each question and structure your response around them.
  • Use clear headings, concise explanations, and relevant evidence to make your answers easy to evaluate.
  • Address every part of a question and avoid unnecessary information that does not contribute to your score.
  • The UK government's procurement guidance provides useful information about the public procurement framework and its requirements. You can also browse live public sector opportunities on the government's Find a Tender service.

5. Leaving the Review Process Until the Last Minute

Rushing a tender submission increases the risk of errors, inconsistent information, missing documents, and weak responses. Even a strong initial draft can lose impact if it is not properly reviewed.

Insufficient time for quality assurance is one of the most avoidable bid writing mistakes.

How to avoid this mistake:

  • Establish a realistic bid preparation schedule.
  • Set internal deadlines ahead of the official submission date.
  • Arrange independent reviews of important responses.
  • Check spelling, grammar, calculations, and consistency.
  • Verify all attachments, declarations, and supporting documents.
  • Submit early enough to resolve any technical issues.

A final review should assess both compliance and quality, ensuring the submission is accurate, persuasive, and professionally presented.

Conclusion: Avoid Bid Writing Mistakes to Improve Your Success

Avoiding common bid writing mistakes starts with careful planning, a clear understanding of the buyer's requirements, and a commitment to quality. By following tender instructions, tailoring your responses, providing evidence, addressing evaluation criteria, and allowing sufficient review time, you can create stronger submissions.

Effective bid writing is not simply about answering questions. It is about demonstrating why your organisation is the right choice for the contract.

Investing in a structured bid writing process can help your business submit more competitive tenders, communicate its value clearly, and pursue new contract opportunities with greater confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common bid writing mistakes?

The most common bid writing mistakes include ignoring tender requirements, submitting generic responses, failing to provide evidence, overlooking the evaluation criteria, and leaving the review process until the last minute.

How can I improve my tender responses?

Read the tender documents carefully, build a compliance checklist, tailor every answer to the buyer's needs, support claims with evidence, and allow time for an independent review before submitting.

How long does it take to write a tender response?

It depends on the size and complexity of the contract. Smaller bids may take a few days, while larger submissions can take several weeks. Planning a realistic schedule with internal deadlines helps avoid rushed work.

Why do tender submissions get rejected?

Submissions are often rejected for missing mandatory documents, failing to answer questions fully, exceeding word counts, or submitting after the deadline.

Should I hire a professional bid writer?

A professional bid writer can save time, improve the quality of your responses, and help you align your submission with the evaluation criteria. This is especially useful for high-value or complex tenders.
